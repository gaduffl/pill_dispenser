Carousel pill dispenser

Ten passive modules on a turntable, one drive unit in the base, a REST API over Wi-Fi. Add a module by printing it. The motors and electronics stay the same, and they plug together without soldering.

How one dose happens

  1. The carousel brings the module to the station.A 28BYJ-48 stepper turns the plate through a 4:1 gear. A flag under the hub passes through a light barrier once per turn. That marks the home position, and the firmware re-checks it every time the flag passes.
  2. The roller meets the drive.Each roller ends in a flat tooth. It slides over two prongs on the servo as the module arrives. Nothing latches, so modules stay free of motors, wires and screws.
  3. The servo turns the roller 145 degrees.The pocket carries exactly one pill from the hopper round to the outlet. The housing covers the pocket on the way, so the hopper is never open to the outlet.
  4. The pill is counted on its way to the cup.It slides down a V-shaped chute through a second light barrier. No pill seen: the firmware rocks the roller, retries, then reports the module as empty or jammed.

How one pill is separated

A roof beam covers the roller. Pills run down a flank on the open side and roll sideways under the roof, into a gap that ends at a wall. The pocket is parked directly in front of that wall.

  1. The roller is a cone.Eight degrees, small end towards the centre, pocket at the small end. A pill lying beside the pocket sits on a slope. Each time the roller turns under it, it slides, and a sliding pill drifts downhill to the pocket.
  2. A gate keeps pills lying flat.It hangs under the roof and leaves a gap 1.4 to 2 mm higher than one pill. Pills arrive one layer deep. A long pill cannot stand on end in the pocket: a 30 mm softgel would need 30 mm of height and finds 27.7.
  3. The rear edge of the pocket is sloped.A pill that hangs only partly in the pocket is lifted back out when the roller turns, instead of being pinched against the wall.
  4. The roller rocks before every stroke.A short turn forward and back pushes the waiting pills square against the wall, where the next one drops in.

Rollers and gates

A pocket meters reliably only when it fits the pill, and the gate has to match. Five pairs are included. For anything else, make_roller.py takes the measured length, width and thickness and writes both STL files.

Wiring

Nothing is soldered and there are no loose resistors or capacitors. The XIAO sits on the breadboard; four free rows serve as rails for 5 V, 3.3 V and GND.

3V3red wire of both receivers, red wire of the home emitter

The emitter in the chute runs from pin D4 so the firmware can switch it off and on before every stroke. If the receiver does not follow, a plug is loose or the chute is blocked, and no pill is released.

API for the app

JSON over HTTP at http://pilldispenser.local/api/v1, found by mDNS as _pilldispenser._tcp. A unique request_id makes retries safe: the same id never dispenses twice.

POST /dispense   {"request_id":"morning-2026-11-12",
                  "items":[{"slot":1,"count":1},{"slot":5,"count":2}]}
GET  /jobs/17    per item: dispensed, missed
GET  /events?since=42      job_done, slot_empty, low_stock, double …
PUT  /slots/3    {"name":"Magnesium","count":60,"low":8}
POST /schedules  {"time":"07:30","days":[1,2,3,4,5],"mode":"confirm",
                  "items":[{"slot":1,"count":1}]}
POST /present    {"slot":4}        turn a module to the refill position
PUT  /settings   {"webhook_url":"http://…"}   push events to a backend

Schedules run on the device with NTP time, so doses do not depend on the app being online. dispensed is what the light barrier counted, not what was asked for.

What stands behind this

Checked

  • No part touches another through a full carousel turn and through the servo stroke.
  • The drive takes the roller along after 4.8° of play, both ways.
  • A 30 × 12.5 mm softgel, a size 0 capsule and a 6 × 3 mm tablet stay enclosed during the stroke and have a free path into the chute.
  • A second pill fits under the gate lying on the full pocket, and no longer fits 2.5 mm higher.
  • For all five pairs the pocket is closed to the hopper before it opens to the outlet.
  • All 20 STL files are closed meshes.
  • Firmware logic in a host simulation: homing with either sensor polarity, slot moves, lost-step recovery, empty module, repeated request, double pill, unplugged receiver or emitter.

Not checked

  • Nothing has been printed or run on hardware. Expect a tuning round on fits.
  • The firmware was not compiled for the ESP32 here; it passed a syntax check with stand-in headers.
  • How real pills behave. That they drift down the cone and are lifted by the sloped pocket edge is reasoned from geometry and friction, not measured. Sticky softgels and chipped tablets can still jam.
  • The sensor mounts assume housings of 20 × 10 × 8 mm. Compare one with the cradle before printing the base.
  • This is a hobby device, not a medical product. It can miscount.
